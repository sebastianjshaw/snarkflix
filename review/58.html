Obsession (2025) review

“The horror, really, is the removal of autonomy.”

TL;DR

An indie made for $750,000 that took $520 million worldwide, and it turns out to be very good. The One Wish Willow isn't a Monkey's Paw: Bear gets exactly what he wishes for, and that is the horror. He wishes his friend Nikki into loving him more than anyone in the world, then ignores every sign she is no longer herself, just as he ignores every woman who tells him anything. It's a film where the villain is the protagonist and the victim is the source of the horror. The cinematography and sound design are remarkable for the budget, the acting is superb, and the only real quibble is that the music store employing that many people is harder to believe than the magic.

An indie film with a budget of just $750,000 and a worldwide box office return of $520 million, this movie has made breakout stars of Michael Johnston and Inde Navarrette. But is it any good?

Its premise, a "One Wish Willow" (a magic spell bound in a stick), is often described as a Monkey's Paw, mostly by those with limited media literacy and an unwillingness to hold men responsible for their actions. It's not a Monkey's Paw, it's straight-up wish fulfilment. He doesn't get an ironic twist on his wish; he gets exactly what he wishes for. There's no misinterpretation or malevolence. "Bear" is just a monster who doesn't really care about Nikki as a person, only as an object he wants. He's a common, casual monster perhaps, one we see every day, one who thinks little, if at all, of women's personhood, but a monster nonetheless.

We meet our villain, Bear. Bear's a loser. When we first meet him, sat in a diner, he's in beige, his hair scruffy and his face unshaven. He narrates a meandering, whiny, self-indulgent confession to a girl, but she's a waitress; he's practising what he'll tell Nikki. His fratboy friend is trying to get him to be less cringe. Quite why you'd ever listen to a fat guy in a backwards baseball cap on how to talk to women, I don't know. The waitress said it was cute and she liked it. He ignores her telling him that.

Bear's devastated to find his cat has got into his open pill bottles and died. (We'll call these Chekhov's Pills, by the way.) But he's not so devastated that he won't head out to see Nikki when she calls. His total emotional unavailability is once more on show as he doesn't mention his cat's died.

He goes to a hippy store to buy a crystal necklace for Nikki for some reason, but instead buys the One Wish Willow. They're under ten dollars and sold in novelty boxes straight from the 50s. He gets a very, very clear warning from the person selling it that people come back complaining, saying they wish they were dead. He ignores this. He asks no questions about the reality of the magic, though. Guess it's known to be a thing. Once again, Bear ignores the woman telling him something.

Nikki tells him he can always call her when he's down. He says "I know", despite not telling her about his cat and why he's down when she calls him, and he's only doing it to distract from his uncomfortable situation at the bar, where his frat friend is trying to get him to tell Nikki his feelings. So there's a clear pattern of ignoring what women say, but using events to emotionally manipulate them.

Nikki tries to set Bear up with Sarah. He hears the bit where she says "If I have a crush on a guy, no one knows", but ignores her trying to set him up with a different woman. He chickens out twice in telling her. Then he negs her and she hates it. She specifically says, "Do you like me? Now's the time to say something." He chickens out again, in fact implicitly denying it, and she's relieved.

His solution to all the above is to spiritually rape Nikki. His wish is extreme: he wishes she loves him more than anyone in the fucking world. It's a disgusting theft of her autonomy and cements Bear solidly as the villain of this film.

She comes out, clearly out of it, getting who owned the cat wrong to try to get him to come inside. She's mirroring him. She's suddenly aware she's acting weird. It's unhinged but Bear's enjoying it, laughing, smiling. She's freaked out, but he's thriving on the attention.

She tells him her dad's dying and uses that to get to his place. She's never seen it before.

She gets in bed and takes her shirt off. He's turned away, but he's continuing; he knows she's acting oddly, he knows she's perhaps drunk, perhaps something else, and he's not stopping. She kisses him and he's super into taking advantage. When she screams he says, "I thought I did something you didn't like." So on some level he understands the concept of consent. I'm not sure we'll see much evidence of this, however.

He reads Reddit and sees a bunch of confirmations that the wish willows work. But note, these people all got exactly what they wanted. There was no Monkey's Paw, only consequence.

She's made a memorial for his dead cat. It's very creepy. He's still not properly aware of how fundamentally he's broken her. She's able to function, going off with their manager. Turns out Sarah, Nikki and Fratboy all work for this manager at a music store. At the same time. I find the magic more credible than this store being able to maintain that many employees.

He tells Fratboy some of what happened, but it's clear he's not really concerned about Nikki, only about how it's affecting him and making him uncomfortable.

There's a really great scene where she's on the doorstep, and just a catchlight in her eyes can be seen, her face in shadow. It's creepy and intimidating and erotic all at the same time. It gives the impression something is looking out of her eyes.

He confesses he has feelings, after she first takes away all the risk by saying she has feelings for him. We then get a swelling of happy-couple music, but with a discordant note running through it all as we see a number of scenes of Bear raping Nikki, as she's entirely unable to consent.

Fratboy's on the phone and tells him that the dying father was a lie, and that Sarah had been told by Nikki that Nikki saw Bear as a little brother. He's worried Nikki's got bad intentions, because he doesn't know the fact of Bear's wish. Bear comes back to the table, seething with emotion, and once again sublimates it and is unable to say anything.

When he finally says something we get the famous "No, no, no" scene. She asks why it matters if her father has cancer or not. "Doesn't matter to me," he says, and it cuts to him vigorously thrusting into her on the bed as she stares vacantly into space, making noises that could perhaps be considered a moan if you'd never heard one before. It's the most flagrant rape we've seen so far.

Another just beautifully shot scene, with her in a dark corner, her pale face and teeth showing.

Whilst Nikki's creeping and her reactions are concerning, in a lot of ways she's showing an extreme version of how sexual abuse survivors behave. She's trying to keep him happy and calm, changing her story so as not to upset him, overexpressing her love for him.

The ongoing fact that he's claiming he loves her whilst she does entirely strange things and behaves nothing like the Nikki he claimed to be in love with puts the lie to everything he says.

She's taped the entire door shut. It's perhaps the craziest thing she's done. Bear has superhuman strength, however, and is able to pull it open. Nikki stands unmoving, waiting for him. She pisses herself almost immediately. When he does finally get home, she's covered in vomit and waste. She's been stood, unmoving, watching for him to come home.

In his lunchbox are two photos, one of him labelled "you" and one of Nikki labelled by her "not me". He moves past it. The meat in the lunchbox is his cat.

Even Fratboy thinks it's clear Bear's taking advantage of Nikki. If a frat guy thinks you're being rapey, maybe listen. They are experts. Bear calls the One Wish Willow helpline. He's not even trying to get the wish removed, only "changed a bit". So he hasn't admitted she's had her identity stolen; he just wants to dial back her obsession so she's not feeding him his dead cat. He does find out the one way to end the wish is to die. The guy on the phone puts the screaming soul of Nikki on; Bear hangs up.

She reads out a disturbing Hansel and Gretel incest fic as part of the dare game. It's a clear cry for help, even touching on the willow branch and the callback to Nikki seeing Bear as her little brother. Things get increasingly uncomfortable as the game continues until she screams "It's not me" and glasses herself in the face. Bear sits there unmoved. His sex toy has embarrassed him. It's Fratboy who insists on taking her to the hospital.

He screams at her and she flinches. Even now, he's trying to force her to be Nikki. He knows now she isn't, but he's still trying to take advantage for as long as he can.

The real Nikki begs him to kill her. Instead Bear asks, "What would be so bad about being with me?" After all this time, Bear still won't listen to what women tell him.

He's chilling in the car, flirting with Sarah, until Nikki smashes the window and slams Sarah's face into a brick, over and over again, with shocking violence. She points out, "Don't forget you wanted this." "This is all your fault, Sarah is your fault, you wished for this." He's still not listening. It can't get much clearer. He helps her hide the body because he's a coward.

He tries to pass responsibility to the store owner. He then tries to get Ian to do a cancellation wish. Ian's annoyed and wishes for a billion dollars. It falls from the ceiling as they watch. No evil consequence. No Monkey's Paw. Just exactly what he asked for. It's the film underscoring the horror of his wish. Ian gets killed by Nikki. Bear goes and takes his Chekhov's Pills, but it's impossible at this stage to see this as doing it for Nikki. It can only be read as doing it because he's got two of his best friends killed, the woman he was molesting is screaming any time she remembers herself, and there is no good way out for him. So he takes the easy way out: not even with the gun, as he can't bring himself to pull the trigger, but a peaceful overdose, staring into Nikki's eyes and holding her, and leaves Nikki to wake up covered in her friends' blood, cradling the head of the man who's been raping her. Nikki's horror in coming back to herself is some of her best acting in the film, the rawness of realising everything she's gone through. The 50s-style romance music over the top is deliberately jarring.

Bear died as he lived: a selfish coward running from the consequences of his actions and abusing the women around him with no thought for how they will feel.

The cinematography and sound design are remarkable given the budget. The acting is superb.

It's a film where the villain is the protagonist and the victim is the source of the horror. It's a really interesting concept, done incredibly well with the camera work, and I can't think of another film that's done it quite like this. The visual framing, with him being scared of her, makes it easy to see her as the source of the horror, but every step of the way is led by Bear and his misogyny and selfishness. The horror, really, is the removal of autonomy.
