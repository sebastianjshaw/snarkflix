Backrooms (2026) review

“The final 30 seconds is probably the best part of the film.”

TL;DR

A Reddit creepypasta turned feature film: 4:3 VHS, very yellow, strong 1980s vibes. Chiwetel Ejiofor's Clarke noclips into the Backrooms (think Severance, or a measurably inferior Stanley Parable), then explores them with no spraypaint trail and a lot of poor planning, in a place that is optional and so hard to be scared of. The makeup on the broken faces is immaculate and the final 30 seconds are the best thing in it, but the rest is predictable, leans on creepypasta tropes and feels like a poor Severance imitation. The most interesting thing it does is swap protagonists halfway through.

A film based on a Reddit creepypasta. Whilst it is perhaps not the most creatively bankrupt concept for a film (The Emoji Movie), it's not a good starting point. Can it recover from this?

The start of the film is 4:3 VHS, grainy and very yellow thanks to the Backrooms wallpaper, the Backrooms being a creepy dilapidated office. Truly, the greatest fear of Gen Z is working for a living (I kid, I kid, at least they're not boomers). Immediate 1980s vibes to the film.

After the faceless intro guy dies, we're introduced to a hard-working Clarke, played by Chiwetel Ejiofor, and his therapist, who's roleplaying as his freeloading wife. He's drinking a lot, and working a lot. She's a student who's taking a break from law school due to the pressure. He's resentful that all the pressure is on him now. He gives a rage-filled rant about it.

Clarke finds a way to noclip through the wall of his store into the 'Backrooms'. The visuals are basically Severance, if you need a mental shortcut. It's also a lot like The Stanley Parable, but measurably inferior, which doesn't mean it's bad; The Stanley Parable is just that good. The film's a mix of found footage and 'real' life. It's interesting, but it's been done, and the insistence on an 80s-style video camera is frustrating. Is that when this is set? Are the Backrooms actually hypermodern, and not creepy dilapidated offices?

One of the more sensible parts of the film is Clarke talking to his therapist about the Backrooms. He's a bit nonsensical trying to explain it, but at least he tries. He then gets his employees to help him explore and document. He's not entirely daft. But he is pretty incompetent in the execution of his plans (see Bobby getting killed, and him and Kat getting dragged down a slope). The total lack of spraypaint to craft direction signs in this place is unforgivably stupid. Even Hansel and Gretel brought something to make a trail, and they nearly got eaten by a witch; they're that slow on the uptake. He does at one point find some paint, but decides to use it to craft a crazy-person mural on the wall, not to help him escape. He also appears to be lost only a room or two away from the exit. Simple labyrinth exploration rules and some X marks would get him home in a few hours.

Somehow, after a bit of time in the Backrooms, Clarke's got a deep understanding of the place. Maybe from the cannibalism. He's also leaning into the creepy serial-killer bit by making her wear a wig made of his 'wife's' hair. His anger, seen first in roleplay with the therapist, comes back in an unhinged rant at the therapist, telling her all the things he wishes he could tell his wife. She finally breaks out of her therapist persona and tells him he's a whiny loser. It's the first thing that gets through to him.

Yellow is everywhere in the film. I first noticed the bright yellow picnic tables. Kat's T-shirt has bright yellow letters. It's a motif.

There are spooky, scary things in the Backrooms, but it's hard to be particularly scared because the Backrooms are optional. You don't have to noclip through a wall and wander around them. You could just, not do that.

One of the more interesting things here is that the Backrooms have power: not 'power from dark forces' or 'power to infect you', but regular 120V (I assume) electricity. There is lighting, there are various things plugged into the mains. What's stopping you running an industrial power line through and getting infinite power?

The makeup effects on the 'remembered' people are great; the broken faces are immaculate. The pirate is just not intimidating: he's slow and clumsy and hobbling around. It does lead Mary to the secret organisation exploring/exploiting the Backrooms. Because of course there is one of those. Reddit's nothing if not in love with tropes.

Backrooms is another film that has you wondering why so few film Americans carry guns, when the news tells us every American walks around with at least two assault rifles, four pistols and a weekend RPG to go to the store, let alone explore liminal spaces.

The final 30 seconds is probably the best part of the film, a series of near-artistic emplacements of the Backrooms memories of real places we've seen, ending on a shot of the broken memory of Mary. If the whole film had this level of artistry it would have been spectacular.

It's fine, it's kinda creepy, but it's predictable and leans heavily into the standard tropes of Reddit creepypasta. It feels like a poor imitation of Severance, and has the world worrying what else Hollywood will strip-mine from Reddit or, god forbid, 4chan. Perhaps the most interesting thing the film does is swap protagonists halfway through.
